A year ago AI was something I read about. Today it touches most of my working day and a good part of my evenings. Not in a dramatic way. In small, practical ways that add up.

## Where it genuinely helps

- **Paperwork:** agents I built draft daily timesheets, weekly client updates and team task plans from the real project files. I review and send. The time I used to spend writing about the work now goes into the work.
- **Code:** an AI coding assistant helps me read unfamiliar code fast, write the routine parts and build test tools that would otherwise never get written.
- **Learning:** when I move into a new area, such as a cloud service or a protocol I have not used, AI is a patient tutor that answers the tenth "why" without complaint.
- **Personal life:** my own web app for finance, health and career data was built with AI help, and it keeps me more organised than any spreadsheet did.

## Where it quietly gets things wrong

The risk with AI is not that it fails loudly. It is that it fails confidently.

- It fills gaps with plausible guesses. Ask for a summary of a week's work and it may add work that never happened.
- It does not know your hardware. A peripheral that looks correctly configured in code can still be wrong on the bench.
- It agrees too easily. If you push a wrong idea, it will often help you build it.

## The habits I keep

1. **Evidence first.** My agents read the actual files before they write anything, and they are told never to invent work.
2. **Tests decide.** Nothing an assistant writes counts as done until a test or a test report says so.
3. **I stay the reviewer.** AI drafts; I decide what goes to a client, a teammate or a production device.
4. **I still do the hard thinking myself.** Requirements, architecture and judgement calls stay with me. That is where my value is, and where AI is weakest.

## What it means for engineers

AI is making the routine parts of engineering cheaper. That raises the value of everything around them: understanding the problem, asking the right question, designing a test that can fail and taking responsibility for the result.

I do not see AI replacing engineers who think. I see it replacing the hours they used to spend on everything else.
